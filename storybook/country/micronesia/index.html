The year at a glance

The Federated States of Micronesia string four states across 2,700 km of ocean: Yap's stone money and mantas, Chuuk's lagoon full of WWII wrecks, Pohnpei's ruined basalt city of Nan Madol, and quiet Kosrae. We rate on Pohnpei, whose mountains catch some of the heaviest rainfall on the planet.

That is why the rating stays low all year: even the best months are wet by any mainland standard. January to March is the relative dry season, with steadier northeast trades, and the westernmost state, Yap, runs a little drier still. Rain comes as short, heavy bursts with sun between; typhoons usually form here but strengthen further west.

Where you go changes when you go

01

Pohnpei and Nan Madol

A ruined city of basalt log islets on the reef, plus waterfalls and sakau ceremonies. The walk is muddy; drier months help.

02

Chuuk Lagoon

The world's greatest wreck-diving lagoon: a Japanese fleet sunk in 1944, sheltered and divable year round.

03

Yap and Kosrae

Yap's manta channels peak December to April; Kosrae offers pristine reef and the region's quietest villages.

Good to know

What is the best time to visit Micronesia?
January to March, the least-wet months with steady trade winds. It still rains most days on Pohnpei, usually in short, heavy bursts.
Why is the weather rating so low all year?
Pohnpei is one of the rainiest places on Earth, with 250 to 300 mm even in its driest months. The rating is honest about that; the diving and ruins are still world class.
How do you get around Micronesia?
United's island-hopper between Honolulu and Guam calls at Kosrae, Pohnpei and Chuuk a few times a week. Book early, and plan the trip around its schedule.
