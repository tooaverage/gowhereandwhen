When to go to Niue

Go in the dry season, May to October: cooler, drier, and from July to October humpback whales calve right off the reef. The wet season, November to April, is hotter and wetter and carries the cyclone risk.

In short

The year at a glance

Niue is one raised coral island, one of the world's smallest countries: no rivers, no runoff, and water clarity that can top 50 metres. Chasms, caves and reef pools stand in for beaches, whales swim within metres of the shore, and the whole place holds fewer than 2,000 people.

May to October is the time: drier, with fresh nights around 20 degrees, and the famous water at its clearest. Humpbacks arrive in July and stay into October, resting so close in that you can watch them from the terrace of a cafe. November to April is hotter and wetter, with March the wettest month and occasional cyclones.

Go for

Best time to visit Niue for

Whales from shore

July to October, when humpbacks calve in Niue's deep, clear water, sometimes metres off the reef.

Snorkelling and diving

The dry season, for flat seas on the lee coast and that 50-metre-plus visibility.

Caves and chasms

May to October, when the sea tracks are dry underfoot and the pools are calm.

Utter quiet

Any month. A couple of flights a week means Niue can never be crowded, and everything closes on Sundays.

Questions

Good to know

What is the best time to visit Niue?
May to October, with July to October the prime window for whales in the year's clearest, calmest water.
How do you get to Niue?
Air New Zealand from Auckland, once or twice a week. It is the only route, so build the trip around the flight days.
Why is Niue's water so clear?
The island is raised coral with no rivers and no runoff, so nothing muddies the sea. Visibility of 50 metres or more is normal in the dry season.
