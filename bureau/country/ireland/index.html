The year at a glance

Ireland is famously green for a reason: it is mild and wet all year, kept gentle by the Atlantic. Dublin anchors the ratings, where the best stretch runs from late spring to early autumn, when the days are long and the weather at its kindest.

Summer is the peak, with warm, light evenings and a full calendar, though even then a dry day is never guaranteed. Winter is dark and damp rather than cold, with cosy pubs and quiet sights making up for the short daylight.

Where you go changes when you go

01

Dublin and the east

The driest corner of a wet country, best from May to September for the city and the nearby coast and mountains.

02

The Wild Atlantic Way

The western coast and cliffs are dramatic year round but kindest in summer, when the long days suit the scenic drives.

03

The countryside and castles

The green heart of the island is at its most vivid from late spring, when the hedgerows and fields are thick and green.

Best time to visit Ireland for

Road trips and coast

June to August for the Wild Atlantic Way, with the longest days and the best chance of clear skies.

Cities and culture

May to September for Dublin, Galway and Cork, with mild weather and lively festivals.

Green landscapes

Late spring, when the countryside is at its greenest after the winter rains.

Quiet and value

Autumn and winter, outside the holidays, for cheaper, atmospheric trips with fewer crowds.

Good to know

What is the best time to visit Ireland?
Late spring to early autumn, May to September, with the mildest weather, the longest days and the greenest landscapes.
Does it always rain in Ireland?
It is changeable and there is no dry season, but rain tends to come and go in showers. Spring and summer offer the most bright, dry spells.
Is Ireland worth visiting in winter?
Yes for cosy pubs, quiet sights and dramatic coasts, though the days are short and damp. Prices and crowds are at their lowest.
