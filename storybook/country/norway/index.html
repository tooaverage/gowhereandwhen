The year at a glance

Norway is a country of two great seasons. The month ratings track Oslo, but the draw is the coast and the mountains: summer for the fjords under near-endless daylight, winter for snow and the aurora in the Arctic north.

Summer, from June to August, is mild, green and busy, with the midnight sun above the Arctic Circle and every road and ferry running. Winter, from late September to March, brings the northern lights, dog-sledding and skiing, with the far north in polar darkness around midwinter.

Where you go changes when you go

01

The fjords and the west

Bergen, Geiranger and the great fjords are at their best from late spring to early autumn, with May to September the prime window.

02

Tromso and the Arctic north

Northern lights from late September to March, midnight sun in summer. Cold, dark winters are the aurora season.

03

Oslo and the south

Mild summers with long evenings and lake swimming, cold but bright winters with a festive feel.

Good to know

What is the best time to visit Norway?
Summer, June to August, for the fjords, hiking and the midnight sun. For the northern lights, come in the dark months from late September to March.
When can you see the northern lights in Norway?
From roughly late September to March in the Arctic north, when the nights are long and dark. Clear skies and a spot away from city lights help.
Are the fjords worth visiting in winter?
They are dramatic under snow, but many roads, ferries and attractions are closed or reduced. Summer is far easier for the fjords.
