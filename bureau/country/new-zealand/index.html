The year at a glance

New Zealand packs many climates into two narrow islands, and its seasons sit opposite the northern hemisphere. We rate on Auckland, where summer is warm and lively and the weather is mildest, though it can change quickly anywhere in the country.

Summer is the one to book for the beaches, hiking and the Great Walks, with daylight stretching late into the evening. Winter swaps to a southern ski season around Queenstown and Wanaka, while spring and autumn are the quiet, value shoulders.

Where you go changes when you go

01

Auckland and the north

Warm and subtropical, best from late spring to autumn. The Bay of Islands and northern beaches shine in summer.

02

The Southern Alps and Queenstown

Summer for hiking Milford and the Great Walks, winter for skiing. Autumn brings spectacular golden colour around Wanaka.

03

Fiordland and the west

Wet and dramatic year round. Summer offers the best odds of clear days for Milford Sound and the trails.

Good to know

What is the best time to visit New Zealand?
Summer, December to March, for warm, long days and open hiking trails, or autumn for golden colour and fewer crowds. Winter suits skiers.
Is New Zealand good to visit in winter?
Yes for skiing and snow around Queenstown and the central plateau, and for quiet, moody scenery, though days are short and the south is cold.
When can you hike the Great Walks?
The main season runs from late October to April, with December to March the most reliable for weather and open huts.
