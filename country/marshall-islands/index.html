When to go to the Marshall Islands

Go from January to March, the driest months, when northeast trades keep the lagoons calm for diving and boat trips. September to November is the wettest stretch, and it is hot, humid and shower-prone the whole year round.

In short

The year at a glance

The Marshall Islands are pure atoll country: 29 rings of coral scattered over a million square kilometres of Pacific, none more than a few metres high. Majuro, the capital atoll, is the base; beyond it lie lagoon wrecks, empty islets and some of the world's least-visited waters.

January to March is the pick: monthly rain drops to 160 to 200 mm, the northeast trades blow steadily and lagoons settle for diving and the long boat runs. From May the rain builds, peaking September to November above 300 mm a month. Typhoon risk is low, highest late in the year, and the heat barely moves off 30 degrees all year.

Go for

Best time to visit the Marshall Islands for

Diving and snorkelling

December to April, for calm lagoons, better visibility and comfortable boat crossings.

Fishing

Year round for tuna and reef species, with the calm early months easiest on small boats.

Atoll hopping

January to March, when trades are steady and outer-atoll boat runs are at their most reliable.

Having a lagoon to yourself

Any month. The Marshalls see only a few thousand visitors a year.

Questions

Good to know

What is the best time to visit the Marshall Islands?
January to March: the least rain, steady northeast trades and calm lagoons. December and April are decent shoulders either side.
Do the Marshall Islands get typhoons?
Rarely. Storms that do form near the Marshalls usually strengthen far to the west; what risk exists peaks late in the year.
How do you get to the Marshall Islands?
United's island-hopper between Honolulu and Guam calls at Majuro and Kwajalein, and Nauru Airlines links Majuro with Brisbane via Tarawa and Nauru.
