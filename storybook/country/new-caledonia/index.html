The year at a glance

New Caledonia wraps French cafes and baguettes around a UNESCO-listed lagoon, the largest on Earth. It sits far enough south to have real seasons: a warm, humid summer, a cool, dry winter, and two shoulder springs. We rate on Noumea, the capital.

The trick here is that the driest months, September to December, are also when the warmth comes back: highs climb from 24 towards 28 with barely 40 to 60 mm of rain a month. Winter, June to August, is sunny but cool, with days around 22 to 23 and a fresh wind. January to March is hot, more humid and the cyclone window.

Where you go changes when you go

01

Noumea and Grande Terre

The capital's beaches and markets, plus a mountainous main island of drives and treks. Driest September to December.

02

Isle of Pines

Araucaria pines over white sand and the natural pool at Oro Bay. At its best in the spring months.

The Loyalty Islands

Lifou, Mare and Ouvea: cliff-backed beaches and Kanak culture, a short flight or cruise stop away.

Good to know

What is the best time to visit New Caledonia?
September to December: the driest months, with the lagoon warming back up. June to August is also dry but noticeably cool.
When is cyclone season in New Caledonia?
December to April, with the highest risk January to March, the same months as the summer heat and humidity.
Is New Caledonia warm enough to swim in winter?
The lagoon drops to around 22 degrees June to September. Fine in a shorty wetsuit, brisk without one. By November it is back around 26.
