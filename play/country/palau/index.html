The year at a glance

Palau is Micronesia's masterpiece: hundreds of jungle-domed Rock Islands over a lagoon so rich UNESCO lists it, with sharks, mantas and the world's most famous wall dives. It is hot, humid and rainy by any mainland standard all year, so the rating stays modest; what changes is how much and how hard it rains.

December to April is the drier, settled half, with February to April the driest months and the northeast trades keeping seas calm for the long boat runs to the dive sites. June to October is properly wet, over 400 mm a month at the peak, though dives still run between squalls. Typhoons usually pass north of Palau.

Good to know

What is the best time to dive Palau?
December to April: calm seas, the year's best visibility and manta season at German Channel. Diving runs year round regardless.
Does Palau get typhoons?
Rarely head-on. Palau sits south of the main typhoon track, so storms usually pass to the north, though the wet season still brings heavy squalls.
Do I need reef-safe sunscreen in Palau?
Yes, by law. Palau banned reef-toxic sunscreens, so bring a mineral one, plus cash for the Rock Islands and Jellyfish Lake permits.
