The year at a glance

Poland has a continental climate with warm summers and cold winters, and the ratings key off Warsaw. The best stretch runs from late spring to early autumn, when the cities, the Baltic coast and the lake districts are at their most inviting.

Summer is warm and busy, especially in Krakow and on the coast, while autumn is crisp and golden. Winter is cold and snowy, but it brings Christmas markets to the old towns and skiing to Zakopane in the Tatra Mountains.

Where you go changes when you go

01

Krakow and the south

The historic heart, best from late spring to early autumn, with Christmas-market charm in December.

02

Warsaw and the centre

Mild, lively summers and cold, snowy winters. Spring and autumn are pleasant and quieter.

03

The Baltic coast and lakes

Gdansk and the Masurian lakes are summer destinations, warmest and busiest from June to August.

Best time to visit Poland for

Krakow and the cities

May to September for warm, long days and a full calendar, or December for the Christmas markets.

The Baltic coast

June to August for Gdansk and the beaches, the warmest, sunniest stretch on the coast.

Hiking the Tatras

Summer for the mountain trails around Zakopane, with skiing in the same hills in winter.

Best value

Spring and autumn, with mild weather, golden colour and better rates than the summer peak.

Good to know

What is the best time to visit Poland?
Late spring to early autumn, May to September, with the mildest weather and longest days for the cities, coast and countryside.
Is Poland good to visit in winter?
Yes for Christmas markets, snowy old towns and skiing in Zakopane, though the days are short and cold.
When is the best time for the Baltic coast?
June to August, the warmest, sunniest stretch for Gdansk and the northern beaches.
