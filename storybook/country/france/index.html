The year at a glance

France rewards the shoulder seasons. These ratings follow Paris, where spring and early autumn bring the gentlest weather, sunny days and far shorter queues than the July to August peak. The capital famously empties of locals in August, when many businesses close.

The country covers several climates, from the cool, changeable north to the hot, dry Mediterranean south. The Riviera and Provence run warmer and later into autumn, the Alps swing to a winter ski season, and the wine regions peak around the September harvest.

Where you go changes when you go

01

Paris and the north

Mild and temperate, at its best in spring and early autumn. Summers are warm and crowded, winters cool and grey.

02

Provence and the Riviera

Hot, dry summers and a long, warm shoulder into October. Spring brings lavender fields and gentler heat.

03

The French Alps

Hiking and lakes in summer, the big-name skiing of Chamonix and the Trois Vallees, December to April.

Best time to visit France for

Paris and the cities

April to June and September, with comfortable temperatures, blooming gardens and manageable lines.

The Riviera and beaches

June and September for warm seas without the peak August crush along the Cote d'Azur.

Wine country

September and October, around the grape harvest, when the vineyards turn gold and the cellars are busy.

Skiing

December to April in the Alps, with the most reliable snow in January and February.

Good to know

What is the best time to visit France?
Spring, April to June, and early autumn, September to October, for mild weather, blooming or golden scenery and lighter crowds than summer.
Is August a good time to visit Paris?
The weather is warm but it is peak tourist season, and many local shops and restaurants close as Parisians take their holidays. Late spring or September is more rewarding.
When is the best time for the French Riviera?
June and September give you warm sea and sunshine without the packed beaches and high prices of August.
